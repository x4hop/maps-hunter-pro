Responsible Data Research

Is Scraping Google Maps Legal? Terms, Data and Outreach Are Different Questions

There is no universal one-word answer. Technical access, contractual platform rules, privacy law and the rules that apply to later outreach are separate layers. A responsible workflow evaluates all of them.

Important

This page is general information, not legal advice. Rules vary by jurisdiction, facts, data type and intended use. Check the current platform terms and obtain professional advice when your use case creates legal risk.

“Is it legal?” is really four questions

  1. Can the information be accessed technically?
  2. Do the platform's current terms permit the way you access or reuse it?
  3. Do privacy, database, copyright or other laws apply to the information?
  4. Is your later use of the data, such as email or phone outreach, permitted?

These questions can produce different answers. Information being visible in a browser does not automatically resolve contractual or regulatory obligations.

Check the current Google Maps and Google Maps Platform terms

Google maintains terms and policies for Maps services and APIs. Those documents can change, and different products may have different restrictions. Before building or operating a scraping workflow, review the current terms that apply to the service and access method you use.

For official reference, start with the current Google Maps Platform Terms of Service and the policies linked from Google's documentation. Do not assume that an API permission, browser behavior or third-party tool automatically grants every reuse right.

Public business information is not the same as unrestricted information

Business names, public phone numbers, addresses, websites and ratings may be visible publicly, but public visibility is not a blanket license for any downstream use. Some datasets can involve contractual restrictions, intellectual-property rights or database protections depending on the jurisdiction and how the data is copied or republished.

For lead research, it is usually better to collect only the fields needed for a defined business purpose, avoid unnecessary personal data and keep a clear source trail.

Privacy law can still matter in B2B research

Not every business field is personal data, but a sole trader's name, a direct email address or a personal mobile number can identify an individual. In the EU and UK, for example, data-protection rules may apply when information relates to an identifiable person. Other countries and states have their own privacy regimes.

That means “it was on a public website” is not the end of the analysis. Consider purpose, minimisation, retention, transparency and the rights of the person whose data is processed.

Scraping rules and outreach rules are separate

Even if you lawfully possess a business contact, email marketing, SMS and automated calling can have separate requirements. Consent standards, legitimate-interest analysis, opt-out obligations and business-to-business exceptions vary by market and channel.

A practical research workflow should therefore separate data collection from permission to contact. Review the list before outreach, use appropriate business channels and respect suppression and opt-out requests.

A more responsible Google Maps research workflow

  • Define a specific business-research purpose before collection.
  • Collect only the fields needed for that purpose.
  • Use category and location filters instead of building indiscriminate global lists.
  • Prefer official business websites for public contact enrichment.
  • Do not describe discovered emails as verified unless they were separately verified.
  • Review the current platform terms and the law relevant to the recipient market.
  • Keep opt-out and suppression processes for outreach campaigns.

Maps Hunter Pro is designed around focused Google Maps business research and structured exports. It does not remove the user's responsibility to comply with platform terms or applicable law.

Bottom line

“Scraping Google Maps” is not a single legal category with one global answer. The safest approach is to evaluate access method, platform terms, data type, jurisdiction and downstream use separately, then narrow the workflow to the business purpose you actually need.
