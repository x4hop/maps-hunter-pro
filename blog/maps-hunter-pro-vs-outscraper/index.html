Maps Hunter Pro vs Outscraper: which workflow fits you?

Both tools can help turn Google Maps business information into structured data, but they are built around different workflows. This comparison focuses on what actually changes your day-to-day work.

The quick answer

Choose Maps Hunter Pro if you prefer a Chrome-based workflow, want to work directly around Google Maps searches, and value predictable subscription pricing for a focused lead-research tool.

Choose Outscraper if you need a cloud service that can keep jobs running away from your browser, API access, Parquet output, advanced server-side filters, or separate enrichment services at scale.

Neither choice is universally better. The right tool depends on whether you want a focused browser product or a broader cloud data service.

Pricing: fixed subscription vs usage-based billing

Maps Hunter Pro currently offers a paid plan at $20 per month and a $100 annual option on its pricing page. That model is easy to budget when you use the tool regularly.

Outscraper's official pricing page uses a pay-as-you-go model for its Google Maps Scraper. As checked on September 19, 2026, its first 500 businesses are free, records from 501 through 100,000 are listed at $3 per 1,000 records, and usage above 100,000 is listed at $1 per 1,000 records. Outscraper also advertises subscription-style crawler tiers on some product pages, including a 25,000-business monthly tier.

This means price depends heavily on volume. A fixed subscription can be attractive to an individual prospecting frequently; pay-as-you-go can be attractive when usage is occasional or when you need very large server-side jobs. Always check the current vendor pricing before purchasing because plans can change.

Workflow: browser-first vs cloud-first

The biggest difference is not a feature checkbox. It is how you prefer to work.

Maps Hunter Pro is a Chrome extension built for an interactive Google Maps research workflow: enter keywords and cities, collect supported business fields, review results, then export the list. This is useful for freelancers, agencies and salespeople who want to see the market they are researching while building a prospect list.

Outscraper is cloud-oriented. Its official documentation says scraping runs on Outscraper servers and can continue after you close your computer. That is a meaningful advantage for long unattended jobs and automation pipelines.

Decision shortcut

If your ideal workflow is “open Maps, research a niche, collect businesses, export,” Maps Hunter Pro is the more focused fit. If your ideal workflow is “submit a large job or call an API and process the results later,” Outscraper has the stronger cloud architecture.

Exports, API and developer workflows

Maps Hunter Pro focuses on practical lead-list exports, including Excel/CSV-oriented workflows and structured results for prospecting.

Outscraper's official pricing page lists CSV, Excel, Parquet and JSON for its Google Maps Scraper, with API access in paid usage tiers. If Parquet or programmatic API access is a hard requirement, Outscraper has a documented advantage here.

Emails and enrichment

Do not compare tools only by the phrase “Google Maps scraper.” Google Maps itself does not normally expose a public email field for every business. Email discovery often requires visiting business websites or using a separate enrichment process.

Outscraper explicitly sells an Emails & Contacts Scraper that can enrich domains with email addresses, phone numbers and social profiles. Its Google Maps service can be combined with enrichment services.

Maps Hunter Pro is best evaluated as a focused business lead-research workflow. Choose it for the Google Maps prospecting experience and the fields the product currently supports—not because of an unsupported promise that every business will have an email.

Which one should you choose?

Maps Hunter Pro is a better fit when…

  • You are a freelancer, agency, salesperson or small team doing hands-on prospect research.
  • You want a Chrome extension rather than a separate cloud scraping dashboard.
  • You prefer predictable subscription pricing.
  • You want a focused Google Maps workflow without paying for a broader automation stack.

Outscraper is a better fit when…

  • You need API access for an application or automated data pipeline.
  • You want cloud jobs to continue while your computer is off.
  • You need Parquet exports or Outscraper's separate enrichment services.
  • Your workflow involves very large server-side extraction jobs.

Bottom line

Maps Hunter Pro and Outscraper overlap, but they solve the problem from different directions. Maps Hunter Pro is intentionally focused on a practical Chrome-based lead workflow. Outscraper is a broader cloud data platform with APIs, enrichment and usage-based billing. If your main job is prospecting from Google Maps and you value simplicity and predictable cost, Maps Hunter Pro deserves the shortlist. If you are building data infrastructure or need unattended cloud extraction, Outscraper may be the stronger fit.
