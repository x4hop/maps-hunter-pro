Maps Hunter Pro vs Bright Data: Which Fits Your Google Maps Lead Workflow?

These tools solve overlapping problems in very different ways. Maps Hunter Pro is a focused Chrome workflow for collecting and organizing Google Maps business leads. Bright Data is a cloud data platform built for API-driven collection, datasets and high-volume infrastructure. The right choice depends more on your workflow than on a single feature list.

Quick answer

Choose Maps Hunter Pro when your main job is hands-on Google Maps lead research and you want a focused browser workflow with straightforward exports. Choose Bright Data when you need a managed cloud API, large-scale concurrent collection, scheduled jobs, webhooks, cloud-storage delivery or a broader web-data infrastructure.

This is not a claim that one product is universally better. Bright Data offers substantially more infrastructure. Maps Hunter Pro deliberately targets a narrower job.

The core difference: browser lead tool vs data infrastructure

Maps Hunter Pro works around the research process a salesperson, freelancer or agency already performs in Google Maps: search a niche and location, collect supported business fields, review results, remove duplicates and export the useful rows.

Bright Data's Google Maps Scraper API is built as managed cloud infrastructure. Its official product page describes API calls, SDK/CLI/MCP access, scheduled jobs, webhooks and delivery in JSON, NDJSON or CSV. Bright Data also sells pre-built Google Maps datasets for teams that would rather buy a snapshot than run a scraper.

That distinction matters. A solo web designer prospecting local businesses may not need cloud pipelines. A data team feeding millions of records into internal systems may find a Chrome-first workflow too manual.

Pricing: subscription simplicity vs usage-based scale

Maps Hunter Pro currently offers a focused paid subscription through its pricing page. Bright Data uses a record-based model for its Google Maps Scraper API. As checked on September 20, 2026, Bright Data advertises a free tier of 5,000 records per month, pay-as-you-go at $1.50 per 1,000 successfully delivered records, and a Scale plan at $499/month including 384,000 records, with additional records listed at $1.30 per 1,000. Enterprise pricing is custom.

Bright Data's separate Google Maps dataset product uses different economics: its official page says one-time dataset pricing starts at $250 for 100,000 records, with volume and refresh discounts.

So “which is cheaper?” has no honest universal answer. For modest, interactive prospecting, a fixed tool subscription can be easier to budget. For automated bulk collection, Bright Data's per-record economics can be attractive because you pay for delivered output and receive the infrastructure around it.

Compare the workflow, not just the sticker price

If you do not need APIs, proxies, concurrency or cloud delivery, paying for enterprise-grade infrastructure may add complexity you never use. If you do need those capabilities, they are real advantages—not bloat.

Day-to-day workflow

Maps Hunter Pro

The typical workflow is visual and research-oriented: define keywords and cities, scan Google Maps results, collect supported business details, review the results table, filter or de-duplicate, then export to formats such as Excel or CSV for qualification.

Bright Data

Bright Data can be triggered through an API or configured in its control panel. Its Google Maps scraper accepts large batches of target URLs, supports unlimited concurrency on the advertised plans, and can deliver results through API, webhook or cloud storage. This is better aligned with engineering, automation and repeatable data pipelines.

Data and export options

Maps Hunter Pro is oriented toward prospecting fields such as business name, phone, address, website, rating, review information, category and city when available in the supported workflow. Its exports are designed to be usable by a person who wants a lead sheet.

Bright Data exposes a broader data platform. Its Google Maps scraper advertises structured JSON, NDJSON and CSV output. Its Google Maps dataset product adds formats including XLSX and Parquet and delivery destinations such as S3, Google Cloud Storage, Azure Blob, Snowflake, SFTP and webhooks.

If your next step is opening a spreadsheet and qualifying prospects, Maps Hunter Pro keeps the process close to that task. If your next step is loading a warehouse or application, Bright Data has the stronger infrastructure story.

Scale, automation and maintenance

Bright Data clearly leads when the requirement is infrastructure scale. Its official Google Maps Scraper API page advertises built-in proxies, retries, rendering, unblocking, unlimited concurrency and self-healing scraper technology. Those capabilities are valuable for teams that need unattended or programmatic collection.

Maps Hunter Pro's advantage is different: less setup for its intended browser-based research workflow. It is not positioned here as a replacement for a cloud scraping platform at enterprise scale.

Which should you choose?

Maps Hunter Pro is likely the better fit if:

  • You personally research leads in Google Maps.
  • You are a freelancer, agency, salesperson or small team.
  • Your output is primarily a prospecting spreadsheet.
  • You prefer a Chrome-based interface over writing API requests.
  • You want a tool focused specifically on Maps lead collection rather than a broad data platform.

Bright Data is likely the better fit if:

  • You need programmatic API access and automation.
  • You need high concurrency or large recurring jobs.
  • You want cloud-storage, webhook or data-warehouse delivery.
  • You need pre-built datasets or broader web-data products.
  • You have developers or data engineers integrating collection into internal systems.

The strongest buying decision is therefore based on how you work. Maps Hunter Pro is the focused option for interactive lead research; Bright Data is the infrastructure-heavy option for automated data operations.

What about lead quality?

Neither tool can turn every public business listing into a qualified buyer automatically. A useful lead still needs human qualification: check whether the business matches your ideal customer profile, verify relevant contact information, and make sure your outreach is lawful and appropriate for the market.

Frequently asked questions

Is Maps Hunter Pro a Bright Data replacement?

Not for every use case. It can replace unnecessary complexity for users whose actual task is browser-based Google Maps lead research. It does not attempt to reproduce Bright Data's broad API, proxy, dataset and enterprise infrastructure.

Does Bright Data have a free Google Maps tier?

Yes. Its official Google Maps Scraper API page currently advertises 5,000 records per month free with no credit card required. Pricing can change, so verify the provider's page before purchasing.

Which is easier for a non-developer?

For the narrow job of collecting and exporting Google Maps leads, Maps Hunter Pro is designed around a Chrome interface. Bright Data also offers a visual control panel, but its broader product is built to support APIs and data infrastructure as well.

Which is better for enterprise automation?

Bright Data has the stronger documented enterprise automation stack, including API delivery, webhooks, cloud storage, unlimited concurrency on its scraper plans and enterprise support options.
